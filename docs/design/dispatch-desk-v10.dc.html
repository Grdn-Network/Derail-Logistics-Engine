2nWhat I took from the boards, and what I changed
Reading the station sheets
The sheet letters are the navigation. A = overview, B…G = the individual yards. The board a crew reads at HB is the same board you dispatch from, so the tabs are lettered exactly the same way. No invented naming.
The station colour strip becomes the station switcher. Twenty codes in their own game colours along the bottom edge, current one raised. It is the one place saturated colour is allowed — it is identity, not status, and every player already reads it.
Symbols kept: switch blobs, track-ID chips, dead-end bars, dashed exits, the freight/passenger/military lozenges, amenity discs. Symbols added: cars standing on the track, occupancy shading, signal lamps, and a clearance highlight for a lined-up route.
Two modes on one drawing. 2a Logistics — cars are the objects, switches are scenery. 2b Control — switches and signals are the objects, cars go quiet. Same geometry, same muscle memory, one toggle. Control is gated on the dispatcher role.
Ink inverted. The boards are dark-on-paper; on a second monitor at 2 a.m. that is a lamp in the face. Same drawing, same weights, paper→ground.
Placeholders: the amenity discs are lettered stand-ins for the game's pictograms (D diesel, E charger, W water, C coal, R repairs, L loco storage, $ shop, ⌂ office) and the yard geometry here is HB's transfer yard idealised — real geometry comes from your track data.

1Workstation concepts — shell, navigation, station view, visual system
1rHow I read the brief, before the pictures
Assumptions I designed against
The page never scrolls again. A dispatcher's screen is a fixed frame: chrome on the edges, one work surface in the middle that swaps. Scrolling happens inside panes only. That kills pain #1 and #4 at once.
Two maps, two jobs. Logistics = the economy graph (who needs what, who can ship). Clearances = the railway itself (where the trains are, who is cleared onto what). They share the same geography so muscle memory carries over, but they never share a screen.
Cars are clicked where they stand. The manifest is a byproduct of picking cars in a real yard drawing, not a form you fill in above one.
Status is color, desk is position. Job state gets the hues. Desk ownership gets a 3px spine on the left edge of every object plus a faint territory wash on the map — so the two never fight.
Still one file. Everything here is DOM + inline SVG, no images, no webfonts required (Inter falls back to system UI), 5s poll, personal state in localStorage.
Open questions I guessed on: whether a crew can hold more than one booklet; whether Clearances needs signal/switch control or only occupancy awareness; whether "desk" is a saved preset or a live claim. Tell me and I'll correct.

1fVisual system. Nocturne tokens plus six status roles derived on the same lightness step, so no state shouts louder than its urgency deserves.
Type — Inter, tabular numerals everywhere a number can change
20 / 600 — station name, view title
14 / 600 — panel heading, haul route
12.5 / 400 — body, list rows, the reading size
11 / 600 — control labels, buttons
9.5 / 600 / .14em — the only uppercase, for section keys
Nothing below 11px carries meaning; car IDs at 9px are labels on objects you can also hover. Row height 26–30px at density 6 — one notch airier than the current page, which is where the eye strain comes from.

Accent — selection, focus, "a human has this"
The accent is never a large fill — it's an outline, a spine, a selected car. Large fills stay in the greys.
Job state roles
availableposted, nobody holds it — quiet grey, it is not a problem
in progressa crew took it — the accent means a human is attached
loadedcargo is aboard — warm, valuable, don't lose it
unpaidrelocation of received goods — dashed, grey, pays nothing
logidispatcher's own empty move — dashed accent, closes itself
stalledthe only red: needs a dispatcher decision now
deliveredclosed and paid — shown briefly, then it leaves the board
Desk ownership — position, not hue
West — IMW MF CP CW SW OWC FM FRS FRC
East — IME CME GF OWN FF MB HMB
Hub — SM OR CMS CS HB
A 3px spine on the leading edge of every haul card, queue row and station header, plus a 5% territory wash on the map. Never a fill, never a pill — so it can sit beside a status colour without competing. A haul belongs to a desk if either end is in it; contested hauls get a split spine.

Navigation model
Lens (top tabs, persistent, per browser) → Surface (map, yard, board) → Inspector (right dock, follows the last thing you clicked).
Clicking a station on the map fills the inspector. Enter or "Open yard" swaps the surface to that yard with a breadcrumb; Esc returns. The inspector never changes what surface you are on, so you can never get lost — there is exactly one back.
